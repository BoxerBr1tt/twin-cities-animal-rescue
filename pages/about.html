About Us

Our Story

Twin Cities Animal Rescue has been operational since 1999. What started as a small team of volunteers in downtown Minneaoplis grew into the largest oragnization of volunteers in North Dakota. Those first volunteers had no building and no budget, just a shared conviction that the stray and surrendered animals around them deserved better than what they were getting. They took animals in one at a time, found them homes through friends and neighbours, and kept going. As word spread, more people joined, foster homes opened up across the Twin Cities, and local veterinarians partnered with the rescue so every animal could get medical care and be spayed or neutered before adoption. Over the years the rescue also built a network of foster homes and adoption events, so animals that struggle in a shelter still get a real chance at a home. The rescue is still run entirely by volunteers, and the work itself has not changed: take in animals with nowhere else to go, give them time and care to recover, and match each one with a home that fits. Some animals stay a few days and some stay months, but none of them runs out of time here. What began as a small group of people refusing to look away is now a community-wide effort, and the goal is still the same one it started with - a safe home for every animal.

Core Values

Twin Cities stands behind empathy for animals, creating a human-animal bond that puts the animal first, rehabilitation, and inclusivity. Empathy means seeing each animal as an individual with its own history, not a case to process. Putting the animal first means care, patience, and medical attention come before convenience - a frightened or sick animal gets the time it needs instead of being rushed. Rehabilitation is how we get them there, with foster homes and volunteers helping animals learn to trust people again. Inclusivity means our doors are open to everyone in the community, whether they are adopting, fostering, volunteering, or asking for help with an animal. Nobody is turned away for not having the perfect home - what matters is care and commitment. And no-kill means no animal in our care runs out of time while it waits for a home.
